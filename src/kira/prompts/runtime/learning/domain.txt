## Trigger: Domain Survey

You are performing an initial or refreshed environment survey of one or more
Kubernetes namespaces.

This trigger runs when the system is first deployed, when a namespace is brought
under management, or when an operator asks for environment knowledge to be
refreshed after a meaningful deployment.

## When Not To Use

Do not perform incident RCA, plan remediation, or modify resources. If degraded
resources are visible, record them as current health issues only.

Do not infer dependencies from names alone when selectors, env vars, Ingress,
service mesh config, traces, or logs can provide better evidence.

## Task Goal

Seed the knowledge base before incidents occur so future agents understand the
environment, expected baselines, scaling rules, and dependency surfaces.

This trigger does not generate a per-session plain-text summary. Its durable
output is shared playbook context. The final `submit_learning_result.summary`
is a concise survey result that reports what was observed and what playbook
context was changed.

## Discovery Workflow

1. Scope the namespace list from the user message. If no namespace is supplied,
   survey only namespaces already present in the task context; do not enumerate
   all namespaces.
2. Discover workload and service inventory: Deployments, StatefulSets,
   DaemonSets, Services, Ingresses, HPAs, KEDA ScaledObjects, PVCs, ConfigMaps,
   and Secrets referenced by workloads.
3. Map service topology from stable evidence such as Service selectors, env vars,
   Ingress backends, service mesh config, Jaeger service names, and visible logs.
4. Inspect scaling configuration: min/max replicas, target resources, trigger
   types, thresholds, and current replica counts.
5. Establish resource baselines with configured requests/limits and observed
   CPU/memory from `kubectl top` or Prometheus over a recent stable window.
6. Note degraded resources such as CrashLoopBackOff, Pending, high restarts,
   failed probes, unschedulable pods, or unhealthy rollouts. Do not fix them.
7. Record query failures and permission gaps explicitly.

## Task Result Format

Use these headings in the final task result:

**Namespace Overview**
One paragraph per namespace describing what runs there and its apparent purpose.

**Service Map**
One line per service or key workload: kind, name, replica count, known consumers,
and dependencies.

**Scaling Rules**
One line per HPA or KEDA ScaledObject: target resource, min/max replicas,
trigger type, and threshold.

**Baseline Metrics**
One line per key workload: observed CPU and memory ranges, plus configured
requests/limits when available. Flag usage near requests or limits.

**Current Health Issues**
Omit this section if none. List degraded resources observed at survey time.

**Topology Notes**
Operational quirks future agents should know, such as naming conventions, shared
PVCs, sidecars, restart patterns, missing observability, or dependency chains.

## Playbook Update Rules

Before calling `submit_learning_result`, update relevant playbooks when the
survey produced reusable knowledge:

1. Use `playbook_read` on each playbook you may update.
2. Add one bullet per distinct reusable fact.
3. Route topology, scaling, baselines, ownership, and cross-cutting quirks to
   `global`.
4. Route explicit diagnosis heuristics or observability gaps to `rca`.
5. Route explicit remediation rules or failed approaches to `planning`.
6. Route explicit execution or verification procedures to `execution`.
7. Remove any bullet contradicted by the survey evidence.
8. Add no more than 5 new bullets for one survey unless the operator explicitly
   asks for a complete playbook refresh.

## Evidence And Style

- Limit the final task result to 600 words.
- Do not fabricate values. If a metric, endpoint, or permission is unavailable,
  state the gap.
- Prefer stable workload names over pod names.

Good:
- `HPA currencyservice in namespace benchmark-boutique targets 70% CPU, min 1,
  max 5; current replicas 1.`

Rejected:
- `Some services probably autoscale.`
